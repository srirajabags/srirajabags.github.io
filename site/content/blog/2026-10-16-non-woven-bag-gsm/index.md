**GSM is the weight of the fabric in grams per square metre — the higher the number, the thicker and stronger the bag.** For carry bags, 60 GSM is light and economical, 80 GSM is the everyday standard for clothing and general shops, and 110 GSM is a heavy, premium fabric for bags that are meant to be reused for months. By law, a non woven carry bag in India cannot be lighter than 60 GSM.

## What GSM actually measures

Non woven fabric is sold by weight. GSM — *grams per square metre* — tells you how much one square metre of the fabric weighs. A square metre of 80 GSM fabric weighs 80 grams; the same square metre at 110 GSM weighs 110 grams, because there is more material in it.

More material means a bag that is thicker, stiffer, stronger at the handle and seams, and less see-through. It also means more fabric per bag, which is why GSM affects the price.

## The legal minimum: 60 GSM

Since 30 September 2021, India's Plastic Waste Management rules require non woven carry bags to be at least **60 GSM**. Anything lighter is not allowed as a carry bag.

It is also a false saving. Fabric under 60 GSM is thin enough to see through and tears at the handle, so the bag fails in front of your customer — with your shop's name on it. All our non woven bags are 60 GSM or heavier.

## How GSM affects the price

Most non woven bags are priced per kg, so a heavier fabric does not always mean a higher rate per kg — but it always means fewer bags in each kg.

## Which GSM for your shop

- **Bakery, sweet shop, medical shop, kirana counter:** 60 GSM U-cut. The goods are light, the bag is used once or twice, and the low cost per bag matters most.
- **Clothing, footwear, mobile and general stores:** 80 GSM D-cut or handle bag. Strong enough for a shirt, a pair of shoes or a box, at a price that suits everyday billing. Choose a handle bag over a D-cut for heavier items.
- **Saree, silk and jewellery showrooms, wedding and gift orders:** 110 GSM stitched bag with side panels. It holds a folded saree flat, stands on the counter and gets reused for months — which keeps your name in front of people long after the sale.

GSM is not the only thing that decides strength. A stitched handle carries more than a punched D-cut handle at the same GSM, and a side panel spreads the load. If your goods are heavy, tell us what goes in the bag and we will suggest the combination.

## How to check a bag's GSM yourself

You do not need a lab. With a kitchen or jewellery scale that reads to 0.01 g:

1. Cut a square exactly **10 cm × 10 cm** from a single layer of the bag.
2. Weigh it in grams.
3. Multiply by 100. That is the GSM.

A square weighing 0.80 g is 80 GSM; 0.60 g is 60 GSM. Cut from a plain area if you can, because heavy print adds a little weight. If a bag sold to you as 80 GSM weighs 0.55 g, it is not 80 GSM.

## Common questions

**Is higher GSM always better?**
No — it is better for heavy goods and long reuse, but a 110 GSM bag for a single packet of sweets is money spent on fabric your customer does not need. Match the weight to what goes in the bag.

**Is 60 GSM legal?**
Yes. 60 GSM is the legal minimum for non woven carry bags in India, so 60 GSM and above is allowed.

**Do 80 and 110 GSM bags cost more per kg?**
Not necessarily — the rate per kg depends more on the bag type than the GSM. In our price list, 60 GSM U-cut bags are ₹260–₹280 per kg and 80 GSM D-cut bags ₹245–₹266. But a heavier bag uses more fabric, so its cost *per bag* is higher.

**Can I get a D-cut bag in 110 GSM?**
Our D-cut and handle bags are made in 80 GSM, and our stitched and digital bags in 110 GSM. Ask us if you need a different weight for a large order.
