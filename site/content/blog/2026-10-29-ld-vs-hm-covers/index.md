## What HM and LD mean

Both are polyethylene, the plastic most carry covers are made from. The difference is in how the plastic is made.

- **HM** is short for *high molecular*: high molecular weight, high density polyethylene (HM-HDPE). Its long, tightly packed molecules make a film that is stiff and strong even when it is light.
- **LD** is short for *low density* polyethylene (LDPE). Its molecules are packed more loosely, so the film is softer, more flexible and clearer.

## Which one suits your shop

**Choose HM if:**

- you bill many small purchases a day, as readymade, kids wear, footwear, mobile and fancy shops do;
- the lowest cost per cover matters most;
- you need a small size such as 9 × 11 or 12 × 16 inch.

**Choose LD if:**

- you run a showroom or boutique, and the cover is part of how your shop feels;
- your goods are boxed or have corners, because LD stretches where HM can split;
- you want a glossy finish that makes the print stand out.

## How to tell HM from LD by hand

1. **Crush it.** Scrunch a cover in your fist. HM crackles loudly and stays creased. LD is quiet and springs back.
2. **Stretch it.** Pull a strip gently. LD stretches a long way before it breaks. HM hardly stretches, then tears.
3. **Hold it up to the light.** HM looks milky. LD is clearer and shinier.

## Common questions

**What is the full form of HM?**
High molecular, short for high molecular weight high density polyethylene (HM-HDPE).

**What is the full form of LD?**
Low density, as in low density polyethylene (LDPE).

**Which is stronger, HM or LD?**
For the same weight of plastic, HM is stiffer and carries more before it gives. LD stretches instead, so it copes better with corners and sudden pulls.
